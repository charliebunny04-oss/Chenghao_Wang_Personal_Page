Volatility Persistence and Path Roughness Across Three Equity Indices

A careful reconstruction of a team project using GARCH(1,1), maximum likelihood, PACF diagnostics and Hurst estimation.

Team Lead · Jan 2025 – Mar 2025

01

Problem

Describe volatility clustering and compare path-roughness estimates across three major equity indices.

02

Data

Daily FTSE 100, Nikkei 225 and S&P 500 prices, approximately Feb 2004 – Feb 2024.

03

Method

Returns, PACF diagnostics, GARCH(1,1) by maximum likelihood and Hurst estimation.

04

Interpretive boundary

No unverifiable 15% fit-improvement claim and no trading-strategy inference.

Problem & data

Move from price paths to conditional volatility and path roughness.

The project used roughly 20 years of daily prices for the FTSE 100, Nikkei 225 and S&P 500. Returns were constructed before volatility modelling. GARCH(1,1) describes time-varying conditional variance, while the Hurst exponent provides a separate path-dependence and roughness summary under the report's estimator.

Methodology

  1. Construct simple daily returns from index prices.
  2. Inspect squared-return PACF patterns for volatility dependence.
  3. Estimate zero-mean normal GARCH(1,1) models by maximum likelihood.
  4. Review AIC, BIC and log-likelihood within each fitted index model; do not convert them into a cross-index percentage improvement.
  5. Estimate Hurst exponents and interpret them with estimator and window sensitivity in mind.

Interpretation & limitations

What the project supports—and what it does not.

  • GARCH was applied to returns to model conditional heteroskedasticity; price non-stationarity and return-volatility dynamics are distinct issues.
  • Strict stationarity concerns invariance of the full joint distribution, while weak stationarity concerns stable first and second moments.
  • Hurst estimates are sensitive to estimator, window length and data transformation, so cross-index ranking should remain cautious.
  • AIC, BIC and log-likelihood values are meaningful for disciplined model comparison within compatible samples and specifications; they do not establish a 15% improvement here.

What I learned

The project strengthened my ability to move from mathematical definitions to an applied modelling workflow, read diagnostic output and revise an initial interpretation when the evidence did not support a stronger claim.
